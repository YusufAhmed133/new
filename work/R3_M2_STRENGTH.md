## 1. Certain defects (proved at source)

**D1. Footnote 34 does not say that Brennan J dissented.**
Answer: "only 'the parties to it' are 'bound by the obligations of a contract'.[^34]". Footnote 34 reads: "Ibid 129 (Brennan J), quoting Wilson v Darling Island Stevedoring & Lighterage Co Ltd (1956) 95 CLR 43, 80 (Kitto J)."
Source: CB14_ch11, cb p 348, heading "[11.65] BRENNAN J: (dissenting) [125]". At cb p 350 [129]: "Kitto J spoke (at 80) of 'the elementary general rule that the only persons entitled to the benefits or bound by the obligations of a contract are the parties to it'". The words are Kitto J's, and the footnote attributes them correctly. But the judge quoting them dissented, and the binding checklist says: "is the judge dissenting …? write 'dissenting'". The rule itself is not in doubt. The majority, Mason CJ and Wilson J, cite the same page at 115 (cb p 343: "Wilson v Darling Island … at … 80 per Kitto J"), so only the label is missing.
Fix (0 body words): fn 34 → "Ibid 129 (Brennan J dissenting), quoting Wilson v Darling Island Stevedoring & Lighterage Co Ltd (1956) 95 CLR 43, 80 (Kitto J)."

**D2. Gibbs J's hedge in Godecke is half-stated, and so is the condition that Grace's counter needs.**
Answer: "Gibbs J, in obiter, doubted that leaving 'further terms to be settled by one of the parties' binds, while allowing that it 'may be different' for 'subsidiary matters';[^9]".
Source: Godecke 647 (cb p 187; Gibbs J, concurring; the passage is obiter: "it is unnecessary to express any concluded opinion on these matters"): "Perhaps it may be different where agreement has been reached on all essential terms but the determination of subsidiary matters has been left to one of the parties." The sentence drops two things:
- 'Perhaps'. The checklist names this exact hedge: "quote or state the hedge (Gibbs J's 'Perhaps it may be different', Godecke 647, obiter and only where essential terms are agreed)".
- The essential-terms precondition. That precondition is Grace's best point: the email fixes amount, date, term and rate, which she will call the essential terms, so the 'conditions' are subsidiary.

As drafted, Grace's counter is weaker than the source makes it, and the source's own hedge is missing. Marker A on v1 marked I.E down for this ("Gibbs J's hedges and the 'Perhaps' exception not met in terms").
Fix (+4): "while allowing, '[p]erhaps', a difference for 'subsidiary matters' once 'all essential terms' are agreed;[^9]". The rebuttal that follows ("he tied that to 'the familiar and objective standard of reasonableness', which 'satisfactory' lacks") still answers it.

**D3. Banque Brussels is paraphrased more strongly than the letter it construed.**
Answer: "Rogers CJ saw 'no relevant difference' between a 'practice to ensure' repayment and a promise 'to ensure' it".
Source: Banque Brussels 525 (cb p 144; Rogers CJ, first instance, Commercial Division): "if the paragraph is read as follows: 'it is our practice to ensure that Spedley is at all times in a position to repay all loans made to it by your Bank', its promissory nature is much clearer. I see no relevant difference between that and saying that 'we promise to ensure that' Spedley will at all times be in a position to repay". The letter is at cb p 140: "our practice to ensure that our affiliate Spedley … will at all times be in a position to meet its financial obligations". What ANI ensured was the borrower's ability to repay, not repayment. The answer's next words, "Rocky's 'I will do that' was plainer", turn on exactly this difference. Stated accurately, the contrast is real and the analogy shows: ANI, not the borrower, assured the lender about the borrower, just as Rocky did.
Fix (+3): "between a 'practice to ensure' a borrower could repay and a promise 'to ensure' it,[^30]".

**2.1 The 5 February call.** The answer cites Gibson 298 (Lord Diplock; HL majority; set). But that is a contrast dictum in Carlill, whereas Gibson's passage decided the case. Verdict: keep Gibson. A switch to Carlill is optional and costs no body words.

**2.2 The email as offer.** The answer contrasts Gibson 298 "may be prepared to sell" with 'I will lend', which is correct. Rocky's argument that the email "merely records the meeting" risks Felthouse 876 (Willes J; cb p 70): a later letter "may be treated either as an acceptance then for the first time made by him, or as a memorandum of a bargain complete before". If the email records a meeting at which a bargain was made, the record does not help Rocky. Fix (−1): "merely records discussions". This tracks the email's own 'Confirming discussions' and does not imply that a bargain was made at the meeting. The 'this morning' further-information clause already covers a bargain made at the meeting. Mobil 498–9 (the Court; set: "the language of commitment") adds nothing beyond Gibson. Verdict: correct, with the one-word fix.

**2.3 'Confirmed' as acceptance.** Empirnall 534 (McHugh JA; Samuels JA agreeing; set) is the best available authority. The words "no counter-offer" carry no footnote.
