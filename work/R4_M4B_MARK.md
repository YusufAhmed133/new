## 1. Holistic mark first (senior contracts academic, rubric ignored): 82/100

It reads as a real lawyer's advice: it starts from what Rocky wants (to recover the money and to know whether he must pay AMPL), takes the facts in date order, and finishes with Rocky's exposure stated precisely. Coverage is close to complete for 1,597 words. Every authority is used for what it decided, and statuses are disclosed where they matter (Gibbs J 'obiter'; McHugh JA 'dissenting'; Brennan J 'dissenting'). Several moves are genuinely good:
- Meehan is distinguished on Gibbs CJ's own limit, not on its result.
- Sundell's 'ensure' is set against Rocky's 'ensure'.
- 'All payments … promptly' is shown to exceed 'repayment of the Loan'.
- 'Recorded' is shown to govern the 4% rate too, so it cannot fix 27 February.
- 'No lender acting for him would need' his guarantee.
- Signature is shown to be neither necessary (Empirnall) nor decisive (Mrs Coulls).

Why it is not 90:
1. **Too compressed.** About a quarter of the sentences carry two or three propositions in note-like form, for example 'Later conduct, admissible on formation, agrees, AMPL later lending on other terms'; '$1,150,000, after 15 February, by AMPL, recorded on 8 April at 4%'; 'even as joint promisee'. A reader has to rebuild the step.
2. **Offer and intention sit uneasily together.** P2 finds the first point 'likely an offer'. P4 then finds the loan 'left to a new agreement'. An offer is a willingness so made that assent 'will conclude' the bargain (editors, [3.10], CB 40, quoting the Restatement), so 'an offer in form' was needed.
3. **The Masters analysis is borrowed.** The classes are framed for parties who 'also agree that the matter of their negotiation shall be dealt with by a formal contract' (Masters 360, CB 152–3, the Court). The answer discloses this only inside Rocky's sentence. It never engages the course's own category for an unspecified 'conditions' term: incompleteness, an 'agreement to agree' (Class 3B slide 3; Milne at [6.10], CB 158: 'no contract is concluded until the parties negotiating are agreed on all the terms of their bargain').
4. **Some counters are weak or misread.**
   - In P15, 'he has received nothing' answers Empirnall's 'taking the benefit' test as if the benefit were the $10,000 payment. On the answer's own framing the benefit is AMPL's promise.
   - Wigan's 'at least when' is used as if it limited the rule.
   - The agency test (consent and purporting) is never stated.
5. **One fact is stated as a fact when the problem leaves it open:** 'the clause, included after Pierre 'agreed''. The same paragraph then hedges it ('unless the clause preceded the bargain').

On a real UNSW scale this is a strong Distinction bordering on High Distinction. It is accurate and thorough, but too dense to read as effortless.
