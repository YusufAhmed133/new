### Pre-submission checklist (verbatim lines most relevant to this answer)

- Before writing any claim the advisee brings, or a threshold that goes against the advisee, write the advisee's case first as a full paragraph from every planted fact, its own words included, with its closest authority, then answer each point.
- Before answering a contested issue, list every argument for the losing side, lead with the one a good opponent would lead with at full strength, then answer it; if words are short, cut the weak counter, not the strong one.
- Before relying on a fallback rule, state the presumption the opponent invokes in the source's words with its pin (Masters 363, 'of similar import' prima facie an overriding condition), then answer it.
- Before resting a conclusion on one contested threshold, give the other branch and its result in two sentences.
- Before submitting, find every fact used in two Parts or two issues and write the reconciling sentence on one doctrinal footing.
- Before quoting any test, write one line on whether the court found it met, on what facts, and which side of ours that result lies.
- Before labelling a bench, count it in the casebook; recall Lord Denning MR's Butler patterns were his alone, Birkett LJ adopts Lord Goddard CJ in Boots, Lord Diplock quotes Lord Denning MR in Gibson to reject him.
- Three-question footnote pass: (a) does the passage or its next sentence say 'Perhaps', 'But', 'However' or 'though'? quote or state the hedge (Gibbs J's 'Perhaps it may be different', Godecke 647, obiter and only where essential terms are agreed); (b) is it the whole Court or Board? (c) is the judge dissenting, or one judge's words credited to several? write 'dissenting'.
- Before applying a dictum, check whose position it describes (promisor or promisee, offeror or offeree) and whether our party holds it.
- Before distinguishing or transposing a case, read the editors' facts bracket and the passage the court made decisive, and set the clause construed beside ours.
- Before applying a qualification, use its own test, not its rationale (Wigan 595: an honest claim, not vexatious or frivolous).
- Before quoting, read the next sentence and keep confining words; never put a judge's words about his case into his mouth about our parties.
- Before describing a case's facts, read them in the judgment, not memory.
- Before citing Brambles, write 'Ipp AJA' (Mason P agreeing) for the majority; Heydon JA's 'much to be said' and revival are minority obiter.
- Before citing Beaton, state the split (Kirby P no consideration; Mahoney JA and McHugh JA a promise for an act; appeal dismissed with McHugh JA dissenting).
- Before citing Meehan, say it has no majority on honest versus reasonable (Gibbs CJ honesty 581; Mason J undecided 590–1; Wilson J 597–8).
- Before citing Pao On, keep its two points apart: (1) Lord Scarman's conditions ('was to be remunerated'); (2) the third-party-duty holding at 632.
- Before drafting, list every verb of who did, said, instructed or proposed what, and match each to a quoted line; any adjective doing legal work needs a quoted fact.
- Before quoting a fact, check the speaker (party or narrator) and read the whole utterance.
- Before using a derived fact, drop it; facts are quoted exactly or marked 'arguably' or further information.
- AGLC: first citation in full with a short title; 'quoting Case (year) report pin (Judge)'; pins after a comma, no 'at', paragraphs in [ ], en dash ranges in footnotes only; full stop at the end; 'and' not '&' in case names (except as reported).
- Style: rule first, facts quoted into the sentence; 'Firstly, ... Secondly, however, ...'; 'Decisively,' once per claim; 'X is distinguishable: ...'; 'The strongest counter is ...'; 'On balance, the better view is that ..., though ...'; 'Further information is needed on whether ...; if ..., then ...' only where it changes the advice. Remove 'cuts both ways', 'it is trite law', 'it is submitted', 'in the present case', 'turning to', 'it is clear that'; no invented abbreviations; no bullets; no dashes, double quotation marks or contractions in the body. One quotation per source per sentence; none over 45 words; no fragments after semicolons; varied openers.
