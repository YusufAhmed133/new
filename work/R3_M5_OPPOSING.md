- Source: CB14 ch 6, p 181, Meehan 581, Gibbs CJ. This is a single judgment. Mason J did not adopt this passage.

  - Met? Only half. Part I's illusory-promise paragraph answers honesty ('which honesty cannot supply') but never reasonableness.

## 5. Authenticity (ignoring the rubric)

**What reads as a real lawyer.**
- Part I opens on the client's question, framed as both his claim and his exposure.
- Facts are quoted precisely, with the right speakers.
- Every contested point gets the opponent's best case and an answer, often a sharp one:
  - 'recorded' also governs the 4% rate;
  - 'which no lender acting for him would need';
  - 'the $10,000 priced his promise that Grace would pay';
  - 'Mrs Coulls signed yet was not a party'.
- Authorities are mostly used for what they decided, with status disclosed ('in obiter', 'dissenting' in fn 45, 'as Lord Greene MR said of exchanged contracts').
- The closing paragraph gives Rocky's exposure as legal consequences, not commentary.

**What reads as box-ticking or mosaic.**
1. **Quote density in Part I, intention paragraph.** Eight quoted fragments from Masters, Godecke and Meehan in 205 words. The Gibbs J sentence stacks three fragments from one page, against the checklist's one quotation per source per sentence. A senior reader has to rebuild the reasoning from the pieces. A5 adds one more quotation, but c8 removes a nine-word one, so density does not rise.
2. **Note-form compression.** '$1,150,000, after 15 February, 'advanced by AMPL' and 'recorded' at 4%' and 'counting only if requested, understood to be 'remunerated', and …' are lists, not sentences of reasoning. They are acceptable in his voice (his LAWS1061 answer uses colon lists), but they are the thinnest reasoning in the script.
3. **Label-assertions on weak citations.** 'offered a bilateral contract' is cited to Beaton 168 (Kirby P on the price of a promise; Kirby P found no consideration on the facts), and the label rests on that. Gibson is used twice for invitations to treat on facts unlike Gibson's. Both pass, but they are the places a marker writes 'ensure your citation precisely supports the proposition'.
4. **No map of Part II.** Part II has no sentence telling Rocky what is at stake: a claim for $10,000, and a defence to the call. A4 is the cheapest partial cure. A full framing sentence (about 15 words) would read better but is unfunded.
