## 1. Margin comments, paragraph by paragraph (in his real marker's style)

**I ¶1 (the 5 February call and the 12 February email as offer)**
- Good. The issue is framed from the client's position ('which decides Rocky's claim to repayment and any claim by Grace that he failed to lend'), and Gibson is distinguished on its words ('may be prepared to sell').
- *Further analysis is required as your statement is conclusive* on why 'subject to satisfactory conditions' does not stop this being an offer. Lord Diplock said the Council's second paragraph, written in the indicative mood, 'might have been understood ... as indicating a firm offer' but for its disclaimer (Gibson 298, CB14_ch03 p 42). Your qualifier is that disclaimer's analogue. Meet the paragraph that most resembles the email.

**I ¶2 (acceptance; consideration)**
- Good. You meet the 'record of discussions' reading, and counter-offer is excluded.
- Use the planted '15 minutes later'.
- The one-line consideration point is right in form and hedged ('would arguably').

**I ¶3 (intention to create legal relations; preliminary agreement)**
- Strong. You use the Ermogenous status factor, Winn v Bull's question at 363 and both classes with their authority, you disclose that no formal document was contemplated, and you give further information with its direction.
- *Answer with specificity to individual Ps*: who is 'the alleging party'? (Grace on her failure-to-lend claim; Rocky if he sues on the email.)
- 'Later conduct ... agrees, AMPL later lending on other terms': whose conduct, and how does a third party's later loan show what Rocky and Grace intended on 12 February? This is asserted rather than shown.

**I ¶4 (certainty; illusory promise)**
- Meehan is distinguished on Gibbs CJ's own limit ('only to the fulfilment of a condition'), and the honesty fetter is put and met. Well done.
- You state Gibbs J's 'Perhaps' exception but not the dictum it qualifies. That dictum is your best authority, and you must state it: 'it does not necessarily follow ... that an agreement which left further terms to be settled by one of the parties ... would be treated as a concluded contract'; 'there would be no binding contract in such a case' (Godecke 646, CB14_ch06 p 187, obiter).
- *Ensure your citation precisely supports the proposition for which it is relied*:
  - Gibbs J's reasonableness point is his reading of Sweet & Maxwell, given when he disagrees with Bray CJ. He does not say it is an assumption of his own 'Perhaps'.
  - The Pace sentence you quote is at 151, not 150.
- 'Likely' overstates a certainty point that, on your own reasoning, is arguable at most.

**I ¶5 (Rocky's claim through AMPL's advance; agency; privity)**
- State the test for agency: whether AMPL, with Rocky's consent, purported to act for Rocky and not solely for itself. 'As agent' names the category, not the criterion.
- 'Grace's promises were made to AMPL, not Rocky, even as joint promisee' is note-form. Say that nothing makes Grace's promise one made to Rocky, so he was not even a joint promisee.
- Good correction of Grace's 'I never signed anything with you': right in result, wrong in reason.
- Tie the 5 March demand to the 12 months it assumed.

**I ¶6 (conclusion; Development Agreement; reimbursement)**
- The non-issue is identified and disposed of. Good.
- 'Minimal' is still a profit. What does that mean for Rocky's priority repayment of his investment once the Project completes?

**II ¶1 (offer and acceptance on 8 April)**
- Good. The bilateral contract is identified, and AMPL's 'nothing bound before signing' is met from Masters 362 and the absence of any reservation of a right to withdraw.

**II ¶2 (who promised: agency)**
- *Ask both questions the doctrine of agency requires*: consent ('owned and controlled') and purporting. You compress the first to 'controls'.
- Deane J at 145 speaks of a promisee contracting as agent. Pierre would be the agent of the promisor. *Ensure your citation precisely supports the proposition.*

**II ¶3 (consideration: the existing legal duty rule)**
- Good use of Mason J's hedge and of Sundell.
- 'The clause, included after Pierre ‘agreed’' is not the problem's fact. The problem says only that 'Pierre agreed and the clause was included in the Loan Agreement before it was signed by Grace and AMPL'. Mark the inference.
- 'If Pierre promised, the exception for promises to third parties applies' is conclusive. Say why: any duty Rocky owed ran to AMPL, a third party to his promise to Pierre.

**II ¶4 (intention: cousins, a company, letter of comfort)**
- *Answer with specificity to individual Ps*: done, including the Pierre alternative.
- Put the counter at full strength. In Banque Brussels 'the facility was subject to BBL obtaining a suitable letter of comfort' (CB14_ch05 p 142, Rogers CJ). Here AMPL had already advanced on 27 February, so the undertaking did not induce the loan. Say why that does not defeat intention.

**II ¶5 (certainty)**
- Good. The undertaking is a promise of a result, not of effort, and it is measured by the Loan Agreement's timetable.

**II ¶6 (privity of the $10,000; 'the clause')**
- Good, both readings with the result unchanged.
- 'Just described': described where? Say 'the guarantee clause the problem describes before Pierre's expectation'.

**II ¶7 (whether the guarantee clause binds Rocky)**
- Name the burden aspect of privity.
- You rely on a dissent, Brennan J. Say it is Kitto J's 'elementary general rule' that Brennan J quotes.
- Empirnall asks whether an offeree with *a reasonable opportunity to reject* took the benefit *with knowledge of the terms*. 'He has received nothing' is not the answer: Heydon JA treated a 'contractual right' as the benefit taken (Brambles 179, his route).
- 'Signature is no test for him either' is true but does not answer AMPL. It cuts against Rocky.

**II ¶8 (consideration moving from AMPL; past consideration; Pao On)**
- *Apply Pao On's conditions to the facts*: done. The second condition is the contest and the timing is the difference. Well put.
- Conclude: on the Pierre alternative, did AMPL give consideration?

**II ¶9 (the 12 months)**
- This is an area of uncertainty, and it is well handled: both readings, 'recorded' turned against AMPL, a hedged lean, and further information.
- State the objective test, and mark the ambiguity 'arguably'.

**II ¶10 (condition 'once Grace repays the Loan')**
- Good. The condition precedent is given with its reason, the commercial reading is put and met by the pricing point, and the duty to co-operate is named and met.

**II ¶11 (exposure)**
- *Advise the client on the consequence, including the bad news*: done, precisely, on both dates and both contents.

**General comment.** This is careful, well-sourced advice that sees nearly every issue. But the prose is compressed to the point of note-form in places: 'even as joint promisee'; 'Even otherwise'; 'Later conduct ..., agrees, AMPL later lending'; 'signature is no test for him either'. Several sentences carry two or three quotations, so the reader has to work too hard. The answer is over the 1,500-word limit.
