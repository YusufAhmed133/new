### CD2. The cited judge’s own sentence defeats the reason given (I.F1; R2; R8)
Sentence (Part I, para 5): ‘Nor did AMPL lend as his agent: a principal is a party only where another ‘contracts as agent’ for him,[^14] and AMPL contracted in its own name.’
Proof: Trident 145 (Deane J, CB14 355): ‘If the promisee contracts as agent for a disclosed or undisclosed principal, the principal will ordinarily himself be, for the purposes of the requirement of privity, a party to the contract.’ Two faults. (a) Deane J states a sufficient condition (‘will ordinarily … be’); ‘only where’ turns it into a necessary one, which he did not say. (b) An agent for an undisclosed principal always contracts in its own name; Deane J expressly includes that case. So ‘AMPL contracted in its own name’ is no reason at all against agency on the very authority cited. The facts that do answer agency (purporting) are left unused: AMPL lent a different sum on its own recorded terms, took the interest to itself, and the Loan Agreement includes ‘a clause that Rocky personally guaranteed repayment of the Loan’, which no lender takes from its own principal. ‘Rocky asked’ is put in the claim sentence but never met on the purporting criterion (SHEET I.F1 core). Separately, Deane J’s passage is about a promisee who contracts as agent; it is a privity statement, not a statement of the agency criterion.
Fix (+6 words): ‘Nor, although Rocky ‘asked’, did AMPL lend as his agent: an undisclosed principal is a party only if the agent acted for him,[^14] whereas AMPL lent a different sum on its own terms and named Rocky as guarantor, which no lender does of its own principal.’ (Prefer the set reading, Mason CJ and Wilson J at 115, for ‘an agency relationship’ if a case is wanted for the route; see C11.)
