### CD1. The agency reason is refuted by the very passage cited for it (Part I, para 5; fn 14)

Sentence: 'Nor did AMPL lend as his agent: a principal is a party only where another ‘contracts as agent’ for him,[^14] and AMPL contracted in its own name.'

Proof: Deane J at Trident 145 (CB14 ch11, [11.70], after the [145] marker): 'If the promisee contracts as agent for a disclosed or undisclosed principal, the principal will ordinarily himself be, for the purposes of the requirement of privity, a party to the contract.' Two faults. (a) The passage expressly includes an *undisclosed* principal, that is, an agent contracting in its own name. 'AMPL contracted in its own name' is therefore no reason at all on Deane J's own words; it is the fact pattern his sentence says still binds the principal. (b) Deane J says 'If', and his very next sentence gives a second route to direct rights (a third party accepting an offer incorporated in the contract, 'compare Coulls at 478–9, 486, 493'). 'only where' turns a sufficient condition into a necessary one; the authority is used for more than it says. The result (Rocky lent nothing) is right; the reason given for it is wrong. The previous version's reason ('took his guarantee') was the right one; it was removed to cure Marker B's consistency point and replaced with a reason the source defeats. 'Rocky asked', Rocky's best fact, is also never met on the purporting question.

Fix (+12): 'Nor did AMPL lend as his agent: a principal is a party where another ‘contracts as agent’ for him,[^14] but AMPL lent its own money, for interest payable to itself, and named Rocky its guarantor, so it acted for itself and not for him.' (If the colon order is also cured under CD8: 'AMPL lent its own money, for interest payable to itself, and named Rocky its guarantor, so, although Rocky ‘asked’, it did not contract ‘as agent’ for him.') 'named Rocky its guarantor' is the narrator's description of the clause and answers Marker B's C3 without asserting assent.

### CD4. Masters' third class quoted without its limiting words and applied 'Decisively' beyond its premise (Part I, para 3; fn 6)

Sentence: 'Decisively, the third class in Masters covers parties who ‘have dealt only with major matters and contemplate that others will or may be regulated by provisions to be introduced’; that describes an email fixing the amount, date, term and rate while leaving unnamed ‘conditions’ to Rocky.'

Proof: Masters 361 (CB14 ch05, [5.95]): '…because they have dealt only with major matters and contemplate that others will or may be regulated by provisions to be introduced **into the formal document**, as in Summergreene v Parker'. The quotation stops one phrase short of the words that confine the class. The classes are framed at 360 for parties who 'reach agreement upon terms of a contractual nature and also agree that the matter of their negotiation shall be dealt with by a formal contract', and the third class itself is a case where the parties do not intend a concluded bargain 'unless and until they execute a formal contract'. The email contemplates no formal document. Applying the class without disclosing that it is an analogy, and cutting the quotation so the premise disappears, uses the authority for more than it decided. The course passage that does fit a qualifier which mentions no formal contract is the Court's adoption of Jessel MR at 363: 'When it is not expressly stated to be subject to a formal contract it becomes a question of construction, whether the parties intended that the terms agreed on should be put into form, or whether they should be subject to a new agreement the terms of which are not expressed in detail'. The previous version used exactly that passage (its fn 6); this version dropped it. Worse, Godecke, which the answer cites in the next sentence, reversed a first-instance holding that further terms left to one side's solicitors put the case 'within the third class of the cases discussed in Masters v Cameron' (Walsh J, 643, CB14 ch06 [6.175]), so the class is not 'decisive' of anything on this kind of qualifier. 'Decisively' then sits two sentences before 'On balance', which is a contradiction in the answer's own strength signals.

Severity: the words quoted are verbatim, so I do not apply D2 automatically; a strict reading of D2 ('an ellipsis that changes meaning') would, and I show that figure in Part 4.

Fix (+12 net): 'Masters’ third class, for parties who leave other terms ‘to be introduced into the formal document’, assumes a contemplated formal contract, absent here, so the question is whether the terms were to be ‘put into form’ or left ‘subject to a new agreement’;[^Masters 361, 363 quoting Winn v Bull] an email fixing the amount, date, term and rate while leaving unnamed ‘conditions’ to Rocky arguably leaves a new agreement.' Delete 'Decisively,'.
