## 1. Margin comments, paragraph by paragraph (his marker's style)

**¶1 (I. Rocky v Grace: the first issue; offer).** The issue is framed well, from both sides: Rocky's claim to repayment, and Grace's claim that he failed to lend. 'Grace's 5 February call asked Rocky to make an offer and promised nothing, so it was likely an invitation to treat' is a good line in the right order: fact, then reason, then result. However:
- The bullets are dismissed as 'its other points promise nothing'. Name the words that decide it: 'Potential' and 'wait and see'.
- *Further analysis is required:* Gibson cuts both ways. You distinguish 'may be prepared to sell', but you never meet the mortgage paragraph. Lord Diplock said its indicative wording might have read as a firm offer only without its disclaimer (Gibson 298). 'Subject to satisfactory conditions' may be the email's disclaimer. Say so and carry it to intention and certainty.
- 'though ‘subject to satisfactory conditions’' is a fragment. It reads as note form.

**¶2 (acceptance; consideration).** Acceptance is well done. The criterion comes first, the counter is put ('vouched only for the email's accuracy') and then met, and the counter-offer is excluded. Two problems:
- Consideration is folded into the acceptance paragraph. Each element needs its own paragraph.
- *Identify the price on both sides.* You never say what Grace gave AMPL for the Loan Agreement, or what AMPL gave in return: the 12 months at '4% per annum'.

**¶3 (intention to create legal relations).** This is the best paragraph. It has the objective test, the onus, the 'project developers' factor, and Winn v Bull at 363 applied to a qualifier that 'name[s] no external event'. The further information is given with its direction ('unless the conditions were settled ‘this morning’'). However:
- *Do not state facts the problem does not give you.* 'Grace never demanded it' appears nowhere in the facts.
- Masters' classes assume a formal contract will follow, and none was contemplated here. Say so before you use 'reached finality …' for Grace.
- The Gibbs J sentence is a certainty point, and it sits here. It is accurate (obiter, 'may be different', 'subsidiary matters', 'the familiar and objective standard of reasonableness': Godecke 646 to 647). It belongs in ¶4.

**¶4 (certainty: illusory promise).** Meehan is distinguished on Gibbs CJ's own limit (581 to 582), and the honesty fetter is put and met ('which honesty cannot supply'). Good. Two points:
- *Ensure your citation precisely supports the proposition.* The illusory-consideration rule in a course case is Pace 150 to 151 (McHugh JA), and it is not cited.
- 'that saved a discretion …' runs Gibbs CJ's honesty sentence into his condition limit. In Gibbs CJ's reasoning, what saved the contract was the limit itself (581).

**¶5 (agency; privity of the Loan Agreement).** This answers the question the client actually has: what did Rocky lend? 'AMPL named Rocky guarantor, which no lender acting for him would need' meets 'asked' on the purporting element. Good. However:
- *Further analysis is required.* State the test: consent, and purporting to act for the principal rather than solely for oneself. Do not just say 'contracts as agent'.
- *Answer the planted line accurately.* 'though Mrs Coulls signed yet was not a party' shows only that a signature is not decisive (Coulls 487). Grace's reasoning is 'I never signed', so the point that corrects it is that a signature is not *necessary* (Empirnall 536). The sentence does not answer the reason it claims to answer.
- Only the party limb is stated. Rocky also gave Grace nothing for her promises, because AMPL lent.
- The 5 March demand presupposes a start on 27 February. Link it to the 12 months.
- 'meets privity' is a wrong collocation, and 'even as joint promisee' is elliptical.

**¶6 (Part I conclusion).** The Development Agreement is dealt with in a clause, as it should be. Claim C3 is identified in substance ('never promised to reimburse anything Rocky pays AMPL'). Good. 'no breach is shown': the reason is that no profit is yet shown to have been made. Say that.

**¶7 (II: offer and acceptance at 8 April).** Lord Greene MR's words are used with the analogy disclosed ('said of exchanged contracts'). Good.
- *Ensure your citation precisely supports the proposition.* Beaton 168 says a price may be a promise. It is not authority for a bilateral contract; Brambles 177 is (Heydon JA quoting McHugh JA: 'an exchange of promises').
- The sentence is 48 words. Split it.
- Masters 361 (no reservation of a right to withdraw) is missing.

**¶8 (who promised).** *Answer with specificity to individual Ps.* The consequence for each promisor is drawn well ('he alone owes the $10,000 …'). But:
- The two questions agency asks are never put: whether AMPL consented, and whether Pierre purported to act for AMPL or solely for himself.

- The paragraph sits ahead of consideration, out of course order. Its two sentences make it thin.

However, *further analysis is required as your statement is conclusive.* 'its condition, Grace's repayment, relates ‘only to the fulfilment of a condition’' says that a condition relates to a condition. AMPL has no discretion at all: whether the condition is met is up to Grace, and once it is, nothing is left to AMPL (Meehan 582).

**¶12 (privity of the $10,000).** This is clean. The criterion (promisee, and no 'stranger to the consideration') is stated, and both readings of 'the clause' are given with the reason and the same result.

**¶15 (the 12 months).** The method is mostly there: later conduct is excluded and the genesis admitted. Both readings are given, with a reasoned answer to 'recorded'. The March instalment is drawn with the right hedge. However:

- The ambiguity gate is assumed ('the term naming no start') rather than decided.

**¶16 (the condition 'once Grace repays').** This is excellent: a condition precedent to the obligation, not to the contract, with its reason (Meehan 582). The commercial reading is put and met with the pricing point, and the duty to co-operate is named, put and met on its own terms.

**¶17 (exposure).** This states Rocky's exposure exactly, as R-AD requires: $1,150,000 once due on the clause, on both readings of the 12 months; damages on the oral undertaking; and paying himself likely forfeits the $10,000.
