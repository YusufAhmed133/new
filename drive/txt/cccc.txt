                                         I.                 JammyJam and Kai




The primary issue concerns whether a valid contract was formed between JammyJam and
Kai.




When determining the existence of an offer, an objective standard is applied.[1] Conducting
textual analysis, the initial message by Kai contains promissory language, with words such as
“agree” and “will”, over “if”.[2] Furthermore, Kai’s query as to whether their terms are “all
good” for JammyJam establishes their message as being capable of acceptance, fulfilling the
objective standard and constituting an offer. However, uncertainty from incompleteness
arises in that no timeframe for the launch (performance) is given in the agreement,
threatening validity of the contract due to its essentiality.[3]




Acceptance must be communicated to the offeror for contractual formation.[4] Whilst
JammyJam communicates acceptance to Kai’s terms, JammyJam’s counter-request for a final
agreement with e-signatures potentially makes this a preliminary agreement; as such, a
contractual issue arises as to whether this acceptance holds an intention to create legal
relations.[5] Because the preliminary agreement contains all the assented-to terms, the final
agreement requested is arguably a more precise rewriting.[6] This coupled with the
promissory statement to pay,[7] mutual assent indicating a “meeting of the minds”,[8] and the
feasible existence of acceptance without signature favours intention.




Consideration requires an objective standard in that sufficient value (benefit to the promisor,
or detriment to the promisee) must be exchanged quid pro quo for a legally binding promise.
[9] Kai pledges a bitcoin sum for JammyJam’s promise to play three wars every twenty-four
hours

for the launch. This constitutes a quid pro quo exchange of a promise for monetary detriment,
lending itself to good consideration.[10] However, an issue arises through uncertainty
concerning the clause stipulating Kai’s consideration. The promise of a ‘market-rate
commensurate sum’ is unlikely to constitute a sufficiently certain description for a monetary

Given the clause’s essentiality to the contract, it is unlikely the courts will sever the term,
threatening contractual validity.[12] This uncertainty is compounded by the volatile nature of
bitcoin, a non-fiat currency with no inherent value, which casts aspersions around its
sufficiency as consideration.[13]




Assuming good consideration, a promise is only legally binding if coextensive with the flow
of consideration.[14] This poses an issue, as the clause stipulating JammyJam’s promise to
play twenty-minutes per game was added post-agreement. Because fresh consideration did
not flow from Kai to JammyJam in exchange for this new promise, it is unlikely the courts
will find it legally binding. Assuming the opposite, uncertainty stemming from the lack of an
external standard surrounding factors such as JammyJam’s skill, gameplay difficulty etc.
would likely render this clause unenforceable nonetheless.[15] However, contractual validity
is potentially unaffected provided this clause is found non-essential by the court and severed.
[16]




Thus, given the uncertainty surrounding payment and duration of performance, as well as the
contentious sufficiency of Kai’s consideration, the courts are likely to tentatively conclude no
valid contract exists between JammyJam and Kai; the lack of consideration for and potential
severance of the minutes clause renders it unenforceable also. As such, it is unlikely any of
Kai’s potential contractual claims against JammyJam will be successful.

The doctrine of privity stipulates only those party to a contract can enforce its terms and
suffer its obligations, [17] with limited exceptions. The primary issue concerns whether
@#@ is privy to Kai’s contract with JammyJam.




One manner in which @#@ is privy is as a contracting party, namely a joint-promisee with
Kai, as then it is sufficient that consideration moves from only one of them.[18] Though
@#@ has no direct involvement in negotiations with JammyJam, the inclusive word “we”
utilised in Kai’s initial offer favours @#@ being a party.[19] Furthermore, @#@ is
benefitted by JammyJam’s promise to Kai given their profit-driven-interest in the game’s
success, making them a third-party beneficiary,[20] and meeting the standard for joint-
promisee.




Compliance notwithstanding, an exception to the doctrine presents itself if Kai is found
acting as an agent for @#@ during contractual formation.[21] Given Kai and @#@’s
commercial relationship, as well as Kai’s explicit stipulation that consideration is flowing
from them solely because @#@ is unable to also do so in the agreed mode, there is a
favourable notion that an implied agency relationship has arisen between them in the
circumstances, despite no written agreement. This potentially excepts @#@ from the doctrine
of privity.[22]




Thus, on balance, it is likely the courts will tentatively conclude @#@ is privy to the contract
as either joint-promisee or principal of agency, and assuming it is found valid, consider any
contractual claims they may have against JammyJam; likelihood of success remains
dependent on the contract.

The primary issue concerns whether a valid contract was formed between JammyJam and
Ninja.




Employing textual analysis, JammyJam’s use of promissory language such as “will” favours
their initial message being an offer.[23] However, an issue arises in that there is no clause
requesting Ninja’s assent to the terms. The message is not capable of acceptance, and thus the
objective standard for an offer is not fulfilled.[24] Assuming the opposite, another issue
arises in that Ninja replies with a new rate, presenting a counteroffer and killing JammyJam’s
initial offer.[25] However, this message similarly stipulates no query of assent, thus being
incapable of receiving acceptance to its terms and unable to constitute an offer.[26]
Furthermore, whilst explicit dollar values are mentioned in both, uncertainty through
incompleteness arises through no inclusion of a mode of payment, threatening validity. Given
the term’s essentiality and Ninja’s substantial execution of contract however, it is likely the
courts will imply this missing term.[27]




Presuming a counteroffer, acceptance must be communicated to Ninja, the new offeror, for
contractual formation.[28] Given the online mode of correspondence, acceptance is governed
by the Electronic Transactions Act 2000 (NSW).[29] Section 13A(i)(a) stipulates the time of
receipt of electronic communication is when it becomes “capable of being received by the
addressee”, i.e., when it is sent.[30] Given no such reply is sent by JammyJam to Ninja when
their message becomes capable of being read, an issue arises as silence is incapable of
constituting acceptance.[31] However, JammyJam teaming up with Ninja potentially
constitutes   assent to their terms from the point of an “objective bystander”, and thus
acceptance inferred from




conduct.[32] Furthermore, it is tenable to assume an intention to create legal relations through
Kai’s promissory statement to pay Ninja,[33] made in a commercial context despite being
between ‘old friends’.[34]

                                     IV.      JammyJam and Sventy7




The primary issue concerns whether a valid contract was formed between JammyJam and
Sventy7.




JammyJam’s video-message arguably constitutes a unilateral offer to the world.[36] This is
favoured by the inclusion of a specific dollar-amount, i.e., ‘no puff’, as well as reliance on a
specific act being performed for acceptance.[37] However, whilst an explicit dollar-value

is mentioned, uncertainty through incompleteness arises through the exclusion of a mode of
payment, timeframe of offer etc., questioning the offer’s sincerity, and thus threatening
validity of the contract.[38]

Assuming sincerity, acceptance for unilateral offers is constituted by performance of the
offeror’s terms.[39] Sventy7 has performed the act stipulated by JammyJam’s offer,
constituting acceptance prima facie. However, an issue arises in that performance/acceptance
for any offer must be conducted “with the offer in mind”.[40] Because Sventy7 jammed
JammyJam neither intending to do so, nor with respect to the offer, it is unlikely the courts
will hold this performance constitutes acceptance. Furthermore, it is unlikely that Sventy7
had the intention to create legal relations when conducting the performance in-game,
diminishing contractual validity.[41]

receives nothing material. Sventy7 incurs no detriment in exchange for JammyJam’s promise,

$300 a conditional gift rather than sufficient consideration for a legally binding promise.[44]




Thus, given the absence of acceptance “with the offer in mind”,[45] as well as no quid pro
quo flow of consideration, it is likely the courts will tentatively conclude no valid contract
exists between JammyJam and Sventy7. As such, it is unlikely any of their potential
contractual claims against JammyJam will be successful.
