What might make a contact uncertain, and thus not a n
enforceable contract?

• Incompleteness
    – Failure to agree on an essential term, or an agreement to agree
• Unresolvable ambiguity
    – Contract hopelessly vague or imprecise
• Illusory promise
    – Usually because promisor expresses the nominally contractual promise in
       such a way that reserves to him/ herself a discretion whether he/she will
       actually carry it out.

• The categories often overlap
• NOTE: courts will strive to give effect to a contract if possible

                                                                                Certainty




A contract of which there can be more than one possible meaning or which when
construed can produce in its application more than one result is not void for
uncertainty. As long as it is capable of meaning, it will ultimately bear that meaning
which the courts, …, decide is its proper construction …
The question becomes one of construction, of ascertaining the intention of the
parties, and of applying it.
So long as the language employed by the parties is not so obscure and so incapable
of any definite or precise meaning that the court is unable to attribute to the parties
any particular contractual intention, the contract cannot be held to be void or
uncertain or meaningless. In the search for that intention, no narrow or pedantic
approach is warranted, particularly in the case of commercial arrangements.
… the concept of a cost of doing something is certain in the sense that it provides a
criterion by reference to which the rights of the parties may ultimately and logically
be worked out, if not by the parties then by the courts… In this case, the contract
itself provided the means of resolution… by the decision of an arbitrator whose
judgment … was agreed to be final and binding … (Barwick CJ)

                           Upper Hunter

• KITTO J. I am unable to agree in the view of the Supreme Court that
  cl. 5 of the supply agreement is void for uncertainty.
• The costs referred to as "the supplier's costs" are necessarily its
  costs of carrying out the contract on its part, and are capable of
  ascertainment by an application of ordinary business concepts ;
  "vary" is plain enough without an express statement of a standard ;
  "in other respects than as has been hereinbefore provided" are
  words of specific exclusion, and none the less so because earlier
  provisions of the agreement may need to be construed in order to
  decide whether a particular variation in costs is within the exclusion
  ; and the right to vary the maximum demand charge and energy
  charge is obviously subject to the implied limitation that the
  variation of charges shall not be more than is required to reflect the
  variation in costs.
• I allow the appeal. (at p439)
                          MENZIES J.

• On its face clause [5] seems plain enough but it is said
  that it is full of latent ambiguity.
• an increase or decrease in the basic wage or the cost of
  coal to the generating companies automatically affects
  both the price that the supplier pays for electricity and the
  price that it charges the purchaser.
• In a real sense, and, in the sense of the agreement, cl. 2
  does relate to "the supplier's costs". It is apparent,
  therefore, that what the agreement does is to provide (1)
  for the automatic variation of charges, in the event of
  specific changes in the basic wage and specific changes in
  the price of coal paid by the generating companies; and
  (2) for the right of the supplier to vary charges if its costs
  of carrying out its contract with the purchaser should vary
  in other respects in the course of its doing so

Recognition that there are questions of construction
implicit in cl. 5, and, that in agreeing to it in the form in
which the clause appears the purchaser may have been
improvident, does not mean that the clause is
meaningless, nor, does recognition that, in particular
circumstances, there may be more than one view about
the true effect of the clause.
Ambiguities of the sort that I have been discussing, do
not however involve that kind of uncertainty which
defeats the intention of the parties to make a binding
contract; viz., the use of language which, as a matter of
construction and not mere speculation, cannot be given
any one meaning rather than another.

How might gap be filled?
1. Every case depends on its own facts – objective determination or may need
   extrinsic evidence.
2. Court will try not to frustrate the wishes of the contracting parties.
3. Court will not make a call when the parties themselves have failed to agree.
4. JJ views will differ about classification of the challenged provision.
5. Court will pay regard to the features of the agreement, the relationship
   between the parties and relevant external reference points to decide whether
   the term which is challenged can or cannot be sustained.
6. Relevant matters in determining these cases:
   1.   Provision may be settled by an identified 3 rd party who has been given power to resolve
        ambiguities / uncertainties.
   2.   If term is so vital that leaving it to one of the parties unacceptably removes certainty, court
        may or may not refuse to enforce it as illusory.
   3.   Existence of a readily ascertainable external standard
   4.   If a contract contains a range of possibilities, ct will opt for the minimum within the range.

Placer Development Ltd v Commonwealth (1969) 121       Certainty and Illusory
                          CLR 353, 359-60                    Promises



Taylor and Owen JJ explained the distinction between terms
which are illusory and those which are real but uncertain.
   A promise to pay an unspecified amount of money is not
   enforceable where it expressly appears that the money to be
   paid is to rest in the discretion of the promisor and the
   deficiency is not remedied by a subsequent provision that the
   promisor will, in this discretion, fix the amount of the
   payment. Promises of this character are treated by Pollock
   (Principles of Contract) not as vague and uncertain promises –
   for their meaning is only too clear, but as illusory promises.
