           Acceptance by Conduct –Brambles Holdings Ltd

▪ 1982, Brambles contracted with Bathurst City Council (BCC) to manage BCC’s Solid Waste Disposal
  Depot;
▪ 1985, Brambles started to manage liquid waste at the Depot and to charge fees on this work;
▪ 1 November 1989, the contract expired, and the parties were negotiating a second contract;
▪ 20 February 1990, BCC wrote to Brambles stating that it was appropriate for Brambles to increase
  liquid waste fees to a certain level (1.1 cents/litre) upon completion of a liquid waste disposal area;
▪ 12 July 1990, the second contract was entered into. Clauses 21 and 22 of the contract specified the
  fee to be charged for “general commercial waste” and required a portion of that fee to be remitted
  to Council;
▪ 19 September 1991, BCC wrote to Brambles stating that it had resolved to increase liquid waste fees
  and that “additional income” should be placed in a fund for the establishment of a Liquid Waste
  Treatment Plant; - [was this an offer?]
▪ 3 October 1991, Brambles responded in a letter denying the contract between them covered liquid
  waste. [was this a rejection?] However, Brambles continued to charge liquid waste fees at the rate
  set out in BCC’sletter dated 19 September 1990 and to retain the moneys; [did this constitute
  acceptance?]
▪ 1996, BCC sued Brambles in relation to the retained liquid waste fees.                              19

Methods of communication – postal, electronic

Broad view – per Bowen CJ in Eq in Bressan v Squires [1974]
  2 NSWLR 460, 461-2. [Pages 86-87 of Casebook]

• Postal Acceptance Rule: Where the circumstances are such
  that it must have been within the contemplation of the
  parties that, according to the ordinary usages of mankind, the
  post might be used as a means of communicating the
  acceptance of an offer, the acceptance is complete as soon as
  it is posted.

  ie: all that needs to be in contemplation of the parties is
  the post as a possible or permitted mode [of acceptance].
