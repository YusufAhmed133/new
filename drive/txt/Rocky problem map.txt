4. Issue by issue, as argued in the final

I.A Agreement and certainty: did the 12 February exchange form a contract?
Rule (1) Lord O'Hagan, quoted by the whole Court in Masters 361: 'if any prospective contract, involving the possibility of new
terms, or the modification of those already discussed, remains to be adopted, matters must be taken to be still in a train of negotiation'
(W3 casebook 155). (2) Kitto J in Placer 356, quoted by Gibbs CJ in Meehan 581: a promisor with 'a discretion or option as to
whether he will carry out' the promise makes no contract. (3) Gibbs CJ (581 to 582): that principle 'does not apply where the
discretion or option of the contracting party relates ... only to the fulfilment of a condition upon which the contract depends'; Mason J
(588 to 590): the purchaser 'must act honestly, or honestly and reasonably', which 'takes the case out of the principle' (W3 casebook
183 to 186). (4) Gibbs J in Godecke (646 to 647), obiter and tentative: it 'does not necessarily follow' that an agreement which 'left
further terms to be settled by one of the parties' is concluded; there may be 'no concluded bargain if a vital matter ... has been left to
the determination of one of the parties'; 'Perhaps it may be different' for 'subsidiary matters' (W3 casebook 189 to 190).
Grace Amount, date, term and rate were settled and 'Confirmed' answered 'Please confirm agreement'; the conditions were
subsidiary or, as with the finance in Meehan, for Rocky to assess honestly.
Rocky 'satisfactory conditions' are not specified and identify no subject matter, so they are arguably further loan terms for Rocky
alone to settle, not a condition whose fulfilment he would assess.
Decisive Whether Rocky lent depended on terms that he alone would settle, which is the Godecke case, not the Meehan one. (Gibbs
CJ's own point in Meehan, that a condition within a party's power still binds, is why the final does not rely on the conditions deciding
'whether Rocky would lend at all'.)
Conclusion On balance, likely no contract. That protects Rocky, as nothing shows he lent by 15 February, but gives him no claim.
Further information what the conditions were; Meehan-type conditions would likely bind.

I.B Privity: can Rocky enforce the Loan Agreement (his 5 March demand)?
Rule Only a party can enforce a contract. Taylor and Owen JJ in Coulls (486 to 487): 'the mere fact that her signature appears does
not make her a party; this is a question to be resolved upon a consideration of the written instrument itself'; 'there was no express
promise by the company to pay royalties to the wife nor is it possible to imply such a promise' (W4 casebook 330 to 331). An agent
must be 'purporting to act on behalf of the principal and not solely on their own behalf' (Picwoods [84] to [85], textbook 290).
Applied Grace promised repayment to AMPL alone; Rocky's request for the loan and the guarantee make him at most AMPL's
promisor, not Grace's promisee. Grace's 'I never signed anything with you' reaches the right result. Nothing suggests AMPL lent as
Rocky's agent.
Conclusion Rocky likely cannot enforce the Loan Agreement; the Development Agreement discloses no breach; any claim against
Grace would likely fail.

II.A Agreement: was the $10,000 contract formed, and with whom?
Rule AMPL is bound if Pierre acted as its agent and purported to act for it (Picwoods); Sundberg J in Pola: 'the consent of the
principal may be implied where he places another in such a situation that a reasonable man would understand the other to have the
principal's authority to act on his behalf' (textbook 290 [11.80]).
Points to AMPL Rocky offered his promise for a promise to pay him $10,000 'directly', and 'Pierre agreed'. Pierre 'owned and
controlled' AMPL; the meeting was at AMPL's office; AMPL performed his 27 February agreement the same day; his expectation
concerned payments to AMPL.
Points to Pierre Rocky addressed Pierre as 'you'.
Conclusion AMPL likely the promisor, because Pierre spoke as its controller about its loan; sue Pierre in the alternative.

II.B Intention to create legal relations
Rule Rocky must prove it, objectively; in Ermogenous ([25] to [26]) the joint judgment noted the presumed 'family arrangements'
and said 'we doubt the utility of using the language of presumptions in this context' (W3 casebook 137). Rogers CJ Comm D in
Banque Brussels 523: 'If the statements are appropriately promissory in character, courts should enforce them when they are uttered in
the course of business and there is no clear indication that they are not intended to be legally enforceable' (W3 casebook 144).
AMPL Pierre is 'Rocky's cousin'.
Rocky That is only one circumstance; the $10,000 paid for a specific undertaking about a $1,150,000 loan, with nothing indicating
otherwise.
Conclusion Likely intention, because the commercial setting outweighs the family relationship; less clearly against Pierre
personally.

II.C Consideration: was Rocky's promise to ensure prompt payment only an existing duty?
Rule Mason J in Wigan 594: a promise to perform an existing duty is no consideration, 'at least when the promise is made by a party
to a pre-existing contract, when it is made to the promisee under that contract, and it is to do no more than the promisor is bound to do
under the contract' (W2 casebook 131). Sundell 327: the supplier 'was bound to' do 'what was necessary to ensure that the goods were
delivered', so its promise was 'identical' (W2 casebook 114). A promise to perform a duty owed to a third party 'may constitute good
consideration' (casebook [4.300] 125, Pao On 631 to 632; Glidewell LJ in Roffey 15: 'a tripartite relationship'). Practical benefit
(Santow J, Musumeci 747) needs, as element (ii), that B 'has reason to doubt whether A will, or be able to, complete his side of the
bargain'.
AMPL As guarantor, Rocky already owed the duty.
Rocky Firstly, he promised before the Agreement was signed. Secondly, if Pierre promised personally, the duty was owed to a third
party. Thirdly, ensuring all payments promptly, including monthly interest, differs from guaranteeing 'repayment of the Loan'. Were
they identical, practical benefit would not assist, as nothing suggests AMPL had reason to doubt Rocky's performance.
Conclusion Likely consideration, because, unlike the Sundell promise, it was not identical with an existing obligation, even if the
guarantee bound him first.

II.D Privity (only if 'the clause' is the $10,000 term)
Rule In Coulls the clause naming the wife only 'authorise[d]' payment to her (Taylor and Owen JJ 487); for McTiernan J it was a
'mandate' (482 to 483).
Applied Rocky gave his own promise for AMPL's promise to pay him 'directly'. On the other reading, the $10,000 promise stays oral
and no privity question arises.
Conclusion Coulls distinguishable; privity likely no bar.

II.E Condition of payment: when is the $10,000 payable?
Rule Masters 361, describing Niesmann: the stipulation 'did not make the signing of a contract a condition of agreement, but made it
a condition of the obligation to pay'.
Applied 'once Grace repays the Loan' makes repayment such a condition; if Rocky pays under the guarantee, Grace may never repay,
given 'might not pay at all'.
Conclusion Enforceable $10,000, payable only if Grace repays.

III.A Agreement: is Rocky bound by the guarantee?
Rule 'The contract between A and B cannot impose obligations on C' (textbook 288, citing Taddy, cited by report only). McHugh JA
in Empirnall: 'the silent acceptance of an offer is generally insufficient' (534), but an offeree who, 'knowing of the terms of the offer
and the offeror's intention to enter into a contract, ... has exercised a choice and taken the benefit of the offer' may be bound; it is 'open
to the tribunal of fact' (535) (W1 casebook 71 to 72).
Rocky His recorded words promised to ensure payment, not to guarantee it; the Agreement was 'signed by Grace and AMPL'.
AMPL He requested the loan, attended the settling of an Agreement naming him as guarantor, and raised no recorded objection; the
facts call it 'the personal guarantee provided by Rocky'. If 'the clause' is the guarantee, it was arguably part of his $10,000 bargain; if it
is the $10,000 term, Rocky's benefit was written into that Agreement.
Conclusion On balance, likely accepted by conduct, because he took a benefit tied to the Agreement without recorded objection.
Further information whether he knew of, signed or expressly agreed to the guarantee.

III.B Consideration: is the guarantee supported?
Rule Roscorla 237: past consideration 'will support no other promise than such as would be implied by law'. Lord Scarman in Pao
On 629 to 630: a past act counts only if done at the promisor's request, the parties 'understood that the act was to be remunerated', and
payment would have been 'legally enforceable had it been promised in advance' (Pao On extract). Textbook 103 [4.80]: 'consideration
given in return for a promise need not move to the promisor'. Australian Woollen Mills 456 (the whole Court): 'forbearance by a
creditor in return for the debtor's promise to give security' (W2 casebook 101).
Applied 'Rocky asked' for the advance, but nothing shows an understanding on 27 February that he would guarantee it. On 8 April
AMPL promised Grace 12 months' credit at 4%, unless the advance was already made on that term; if 'the clause' is the guarantee,
AMPL also arguably promised Rocky $10,000 for it.
Conflict If 8 April created the term, as Rocky's timing argument requires, AMPL gave fresh consideration that day.
Conclusion Likely supported, because AMPL's 8 April promises, not the past advance, were given in exchange for it.

III.C Certainty and construction: was the 9 March call premature?
Rule Gibbs CJ in Meehan 578: 'If the court, in construing the contract, can decide which of the two possible meanings is that which
the parties intended, there will be no uncertainty'. Heydon JA in Brambles 163: pre-contractual conduct is admissible on construction
only 'if the contract is ambiguous' and it 'casts light on the genesis of the contract' (Ipp AJA 191: 'mutually known facts'); 164: 'post-
contractual conduct is not admissible on the question of what a contract means' (W1 casebook 73).
Rocky The Agreement created the term; nothing shows a term was agreed on 27 February, when 'Pierre agreed to provide the money
to Grace'.
AMPL The loan 'was recorded as $1,150,000', so the Agreement recorded money already lent, and the advance, known to all three,
shows the term ran from 27 February.
Excluded Later conduct, including the interest payments and Rocky's own 5 March demand.
Conclusion Term certain, running from 8 April 2024, because the Agreement created the term and the advance shows only when the
money was lent; 'recorded' describes the amount, not the start of the term. Further information whether the Agreement tied the
term or interest to the advance; if it did, the call was timely and Rocky likely also breached his promise to ensure prompt payment.

  Advice to Rocky He has no contractual claim against Grace. He likely holds an enforceable promise of $10,000 from AMPL (alternatively
  Pierre), payable only if Grace repays. He is likely bound by the guarantee, but its 12 months likely run from 8 April 2024, so he should resist
  AMPL's 9 March 2025 call; paying under the guarantee would probably mean the $10,000 condition is never fulfilled.
