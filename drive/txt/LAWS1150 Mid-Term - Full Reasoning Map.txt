4. BOTTOM LINE
    1. Rocky has no contractual claim against Grace (beyond the Development Agreement, which is not shown breached): the email was probably not binding because the loan was left ‘subject to
       satisfactory conditions’ that Rocky alone would settle (P3, P4), and his 5 March demand fails on privity because Grace’s promises in the Loan Agreement were made to AMPL, not to him,
       and AMPL lent for itself, not as his agent (P5, P6).
    2. For the same reason Grace probably cannot sue Rocky for failing to lend by 15 February (P1 to P4, P6).
    3. Rocky probably made a binding oral contract with AMPL (or, if Pierre spoke for himself, with Pierre): he promised that Grace would make all payments promptly, in return for $10,000 (P7
       to P11).
    4. Rocky is probably liable to AMPL at least on that oral undertaking if Grace does not pay when due, and for $1,150,000 under the written clause only if he assented to it, which is arguable
       but not certain because he signed nothing (P12, P13, P16).
    5. Whether AMPL’s 9 March 2025 call was premature turns on whether the 12 months ran from 27 February 2024 (call timely) or 8 April 2024 (call premature until 8 April 2025); this is open
       and depends on the Loan Agreement’s own words (P14, P16).
    6. Rocky’s $10,000 is owed only once Grace repays, so it is likely never payable if Grace defaults and Rocky pays instead (P15, P16).
