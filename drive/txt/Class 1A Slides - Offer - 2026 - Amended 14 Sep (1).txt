   – Eg: Cannot be an offer if there is not an intention to create

                                                                    11
                        UNILATERAL contract cf a BILATERAL
                        contract - differences                        Agreement: Offer




• Differences in how each is created
• Bilateral contract – contract is made by exchange of promises to do
  something – then the parties do what they agreed– eg Gibson v
  Manchester City Council
    – When first made, a bilateral contract will be executory – that refers to
      stage of contract performance
• Unilateral contract – one in which the act of acceptance of the offer
  is also the performance – see Mobil Oil Australia v Wellcome
  International; Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Company cases
• Offer is made by the offeror and is accepted by doing or refraining
  to do the act that is requested by them – eg the full ct in Mobil Oil –
  eg of the offer of a reward for return of a lost item
• There is no exchange of promises, the offeree has a choice of
  whether to perform. If they do perform then the offeror is bound to
  perform

  MacRobertson Miller Airline Services v Commissioner of
         State Taxation (WA) (1975) 133 CLR 125                                 Agreement: Offer


• Passenger was told the availability of fare, paid for it and was handed a ticket
  that contained conditions – then on day of flight they turned up to take flight …

• When was the contract formed?

• Stephen J: looked at acceptance
    – ? When customer paid fare
    – ? When airline handed ticket to customer
    – Ticket contains terms and conditions – how can passenger be taken to have read and
      agreed to the terms before they received the printed ticket?
    – A different analysis has been developed by ct:
    – Ticket seen as offer
    – Contract made on acceptance of that offer by passenger
    – In this situation there was a delay between getting ticket and taking flight – the passenger
      had a chance to read the conditions and accept or reject them
    – So acceptance occurs by the passenger’s conduct – by boarding the flight or when they
      have had a reasonable time to read the conditions and have not rejected the offer

MacRobertson Miller Airline Services v Commissioner of
               State Taxation (WA)
• Barwick CJ – was concerned with consideration
• Consideration = both parties must bring something valuable to the
  bargain
• It must be a promise to do something valuable, not illusory (wk 2)
• He found the airline was making an illusory promise
• It did not assume any obligation to carry the passenger – ‘we
  promise to fly you unless we choose not to’
• There was no promise, therefore no contract
• Airline not in a contractual situation with the passenger until they
  flew the passenger

      Does an offer, once made, last for ever?
• In general, can an offeror withdraw an offer before the expiry
  of the specified open period of the offer?
• Yes, at that stage you do not have a binding contract unless
  the offer has been accepted.

How can an offer come to an end?
1. Lapse after X time or a reasonable time if nothing stipulated
2. A failure of a condition
3. Where an offer has been rejected by the other party –
   rejection terminates the offer, not available to be accepted
   any longer
4. Offer can be withdrawn or revoked any time to when it is
   accepted – even if you had promised to keep the offer open -
   BUT – see next slide

Revision of basic concepts


Concept               Definition
Offer                 An expression of willingness to enter into a contract on specific
                      terms.
Invitation to treat   An invitation for others to make an offer or enter into a
                      negotiation.
Unilateral contract   A contract in which the offeree accepts the offer by performing
                      their side of the bargain. It is unilateral because one party has
                      performed his or her obligations by the time of formation, only
                      the other party is ever under a contractual obligation.
Bilateral contract    A contract in which both parties are under a contractual
                      obligation to perform their side of the bargain at the time of
                      formation.
