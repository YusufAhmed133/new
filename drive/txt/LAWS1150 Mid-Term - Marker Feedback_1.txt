II.F2). Mason and Jacobs JJ at 269 were in the High Court majority that the Privy Council reversed in The New York Star (1980) 144
CLR 300, where ‘their Lordships are in agreement with the judgment of the Chief Justice’ (official PC text [28]). Lord Reid’s
requisites survive, so the proposition is sound, and the answer correctly flags ‘rev’d’. Still, Barwick CJ at 240 states the same
requisites and is the judgment the Privy Council adopted. Fix: ‘Port Jackson Stevedoring Pty Ltd v Salmond & Spraggon (Aust) Pty

after the ‘, quoting’ clause is also open to argument, because the subsequent history belongs to Port Jackson, not to Scruttons.

K9. ‘as his check-ins did’ (II.E) is unclear. It reads as though post-contractual conduct is being used to fix what Rocky promised,
which the answer itself rules out at II.G (Brambles 164). This is an R1 clarity lapse. Fix: delete it (minus 4); it is used to fund C2.

K10. Two planted facts on the 12 months are not used (II.G). These are the ‘appropriate monthly repayments of the interest’
from 8 April 2024 to 8 February 2025, and Rocky’s own 13 February 2025 email and 5 March 2025 demand. ‘Post-contractual
conduct cannot interpret the term’ disposes of them correctly in law, but a strict marker wants the facts named and set aside (R-

to its meaning,’ (plus 14).

K11. ‘The clause’ is read only one way (II.F1; R-M). The answer assumes throughout that the clause included before signing
was the guarantee. If it was the $10,000 promise, the written clause is in AMPL and Grace’s contract, and Rocky could not enforce it

K12. Rocky’s answer at II.I2 is put but never met. The paragraph ends on ‘Rocky would answer that the $10,000 priced his own

K13. Gibbs J’s dictum is obiter, and the answer relies on it to decide intention (I.D; R-F). ‘Gibbs J doubted’ signals that it is
tentative, but the dictum carries part of the decision. Fix: ‘Gibbs J doubted, obiter, that …’ (plus 1).

K14. Privity is stated as a single rule (fn 13). ‘Only a party who provided consideration may enforce a contract’ merges the two
requirements, whereas the casebook at [11.10] says the privity rule ‘is separate from the rule that consideration must move from the
promisee’. Windeyer J at 495 (‘Doubtless the two requisites merge in the strict view of a contract as a bargain’) supports the merged
form, so this is defensible. Fix if wanted: ‘… only a party may enforce a contract, consideration also having to move from that party’
(plus 3).

K15. Pace 150 to 151 is not cited, and Beaton 181 and Musumeci 745 are absent (I.E, II.C1; required authorities). Each
